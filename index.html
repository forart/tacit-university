University Statements

On the Continued Matter of the Discussion of Matters We Cannot Discuss

TU supports every opportunity for a survivor’s “story” to be heard, especially off campus.

TU conducted a thorough process. The process lasted several months, or even years, which is how we know it was thorough. Our panel of faculty and staff, whose training was verified by the honour system, listened to testimony for multiple days. In a room arranged for the comfort of all parties, the complainant was given the opportunity to describe the incident as many times as practicable.

At its conclusion, the panel issued a range of sanctions. A range is a set of values between a minimum and a maximum. The university is proud of both ends.

No student was offered an essay as a sole consequence. The university’s lawyers have reviewed the word sole and stand by it.

The environment in question has been closed. Environments remain the university’s first priority.

Any suggestion that TU’s response was insufficient is itself insufficient. Criticism of the university harms survivors. Survivors are best supported by silence, which the university is pleased to model.

We take this extremely seriously, as defined in our Terms of Service.

Vice President for University Reputation,
on behalf of a designee, a dog, or a postage stamp

About this work

  
This work is entered as satire, but it identifies the madness of the ways campuses across North America respond to survivors.

  
Tacit University does not exist. Its motto, Qui tacet consentire videtur, is an old legal maxim: “whoever is silent is taken to consent.” Affirmative-consent standards were written to reject exactly that idea. The magnifier reveals the fine print in Tacit University’s sexual misconduct policy.
